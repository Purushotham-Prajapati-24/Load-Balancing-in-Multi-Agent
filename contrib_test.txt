This paper introduces the Hybrid Adaptive Scheduler (HAS), an innovative approach that effectively reconciles the divide between offline and online paradigms by employing a tunable batch parameter alpha. Instead of adhering strictly to one methodology, HAS temporarily buffers a specific fraction alpha of incoming tasks, applies an LPT-based scheduling logic to this batch, and subsequently manages the remaining tasks in an online fashion. Our primary contributions include: A detailed formalization of the HAS algorithm along with a detailed complexity analysis. A derivation of theoretical competitive ratio bounds expressed as a function of the parameter alpha. An extensive experimental evaluation across three distinct machine models P, Q, and R, highlighting the practical advantages of our hybrid approach over traditional greedy scheduling. A sensitivity analysis of the alpha parameter to identify optimal batching strategies for varied workload profiles.
