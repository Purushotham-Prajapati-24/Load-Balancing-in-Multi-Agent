
Within the context of multi-agent systems (MAS), the pursuit of efficient task scheduling remains a critical objective for minimizing makespan---defined as the cumulative duration required to finalize all tasks across a pool of heterogeneous agents with diverse computational strengths. Traditional offline approaches, such as the Longest Processing Time (LPT) rule, are capable of achieving tight $(4/3 - 1/3M)$ approximation ratios. However, such methods are fundamentally constrained by the requirement for exhaustive, a priori knowledge of all tasks. In contrast, online greedy schedulers offer the benefit of instantaneous task assignment, though this responsiveness often comes at the cost of degraded $O(\log n)$ competitive ratios when operating on heterogeneous machine models. To address this trade-off, we introduce the \textbf{Hybrid Adaptive Scheduler (HAS)}, a parameterized algorithmic framework that leverages a batch fraction $\alpha \in [0,1]$ to interpolate between the strengths of offline LPT and online greedy strategies. Our theoretical analysis demonstrates that HAS yields a competitive ratio which monotonically strengthens as $\alpha$ increases, effectively converging toward the offline bound as the batch size grows. Through a series of rigorous evaluations spanning identical ($P||C_{\max}$), uniform ($Q||C_{\max}$), and unrelated ($R||C_{\max}$) machine models, we show that HAS---specifically with a batch fraction of $\alpha = 0.5$---maintains competitive ratios within 3\% of the offline optimal. Remarkably, this is achieved while preserving the real-time responsiveness characteristic of online systems. Ultimately, these findings offer concrete, practical insights for architects of multi-agent systems who must navigate the delicate balance between scheduling precision and operational latency.
